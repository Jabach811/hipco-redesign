Health, safety, and environment

Nobody gets hurt. Nothing gets wasted.

We move chemicals, run forklifts, and put trucks on the road every day. That is a lot of ways to hurt someone or the ground under us. Here is how we keep both from happening, and what we are measuring ourselves against.

Safety

The first job on every job.

A branch that runs safely runs on time. An injury stops the counter, the truck, and the customer's project all at once.

Safety at Harrington is not a poster in the break room. It is how a warehouse is laid out, how a drum of acid is stored, how a driver is trained before the first delivery, and what a branch manager checks before anyone else clocks in. Every location works from the same rules, and every associate has the standing to stop a job that looks wrong.

It covers the customer's site too. When our people walk a plant to size a line or commission a pump, they follow that plant's rules and bring our own. And it covers the neighborhoods around our branches, where our trucks share the road and our tanks share the ground.

What we are protecting
Our associates first, then our customers and the communities where we operate.
What we expect
Industry-leading results for customers, earned without shortcuts on the safety of the team delivering them.

Environment

Targets we publish, not intentions we announce.

Our impact runs from the factories that make what we sell to the plants that install it. We hold ourselves and our suppliers to the same list.

Read the policy statement (PDF)

A distributor does not run smokestacks, but it runs trucks, warehouses, packaging, and a supply chain of more than 120 manufacturers. That is where our footprint lives, and it is where we work on it. The focus is climate, emissions, and waste, in that order.

What we have committed to

  • Meet every environmental law where we operate, then set a higher bar for ourselves and for the suppliers we buy from.
  • Put numbers on our environmental impact and greenhouse gas reduction, share them openly, and report progress in public, not just internally.
  • Measure impact across the whole value chain and set reduction plans that hold up while the company keeps growing.
  • Treat environmental risk as business risk. It sits in the company-wide risk review and goes in front of the executive team.
  • Run recognized environmental management systems, have them audited by outside parties, and certify locations where certification exists.
  • Give associates the training and tools to act on all of this, so the policy is something people do rather than something they read.
  • Work with suppliers on their own footprint: carbon reduction, water use, waste plans, and training.

Climate

15% less by 2030. Net zero by 2040.

Science-based targets in line with the Paris Agreement, with the near-term work already under way.

We have joined the Net Zero movement and set a science-based target to cut our greenhouse gas emissions 15% by 2030. After 2030 the targets keep tightening along a net zero path that ends in 2040. Three things get us there.

How we get there

  • Our own operations. Move facilities to 100% renewable electricity. Electrify the delivery fleet wherever a route and a charger make it practical. Fix the inefficiencies we can see in our own buildings and yards.
  • Our suppliers. Most of the emissions behind a valve or a length of pipe happen before it reaches our shelf. We work with manufacturers on energy efficiency and renewable sourcing, and help the ones without a climate plan build one that lines up with ours.
  • The products themselves. Recycling, repair, and resale programs that keep equipment in service longer, so less virgin material gets made and less usable gear goes in a dumpster. Our pump repair shops are the working example.

Supplier questionnaires, safety records, and emissions reporting. Tell us what your procurement team needs and we will route it.
