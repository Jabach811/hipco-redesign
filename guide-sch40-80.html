Schedule 40 vs Schedule 80 PVC

Same outside diameter, different wall. When the thicker pipe is worth it and when it is not.

Schedule 40 and Schedule 80 refer to standardized wall thickness ratings for pipe, most commonly used in PVC and steel piping systems. Both share the same outside diameter for a given pipe size. Schedule 80 has thicker walls, making it stronger and better suited to higher-pressure applications. Schedule 40, with its thinner walls, is lighter, more cost-effective, and typically used in lower-pressure or general-purpose systems. The choice between the two depends on pressure requirements, durability needs, and overall system design.

Wall thickness

PVC is a high-strength thermoplastic widely used in drainage, water and wastewater treatment, irrigation, agriculture, chemical handling, vent tubing, ductwork, and other corrosive fluid transfer. It is imperative that the correct wall thickness, or pipe schedule, is used for your project.

As the schedule number increases, the pipe wall becomes thicker and the inside diameter smaller. Consequently, Schedule 80 pipe will have slightly more restricted flow than Schedule 40 pipe of the same outside diameter. Schedule 40 PVC is usually white; Schedule 80 is usually dark gray.

PVC Schedule 40

Schedule 40 has thinner walls, so it is best for applications involving relatively low water pressure: drainage, irrigation, and other cold-water systems. It has a maximum operating temperature of 140°F (60°C), is usually white, and is commonly found in local hardware stores.

  • Sizes: typically 1/2 in to 24 in diameter.
  • Pressure rating: up to 450 psi depending on size and temperature.
  • Temperature: up to 140°F (60°C). Performance varies with temperature.
  • Applications: potable water, irrigation, drainage, electrical conduit, and industrial fluid transport.
  • Installation: easy to cut, join, and install with solvent cement, primer, and fittings.
  • Durability: lifespan over 50 years under normal conditions.
  • Compliance: ASTM D1785 and NSF standards for potable water.
  • UV resistance: limited. May require painting or other protection for prolonged outdoor use.

PVC Schedule 80

Schedule 80 has a thicker wall than Schedule 40 of the same outside diameter. It is commonly used for high-pressure, industrial, and chemical applications. Because of the thicker wall, it is heavier than comparable Schedule 40 pipe and fittings, and it is usually gray to tell it apart.

  • Sizes: typically 1/2 in to 24 in diameter.
  • Pressure rating: generally up to 850 psi depending on size and temperature.
  • Temperature: up to 140°F (60°C). Pressure capacity decreases at higher temperatures.
  • Applications: high-pressure water, chemical processing, heavy-duty industrial, and electrical conduit in high-stress environments.
  • Installation: solvent cement, primer, and compatible fittings. Schedule 80 can also be threaded.
  • Durability: lifespan often exceeding 50 years under normal conditions.
  • Cost: more expensive than Schedule 40 due to thicker walls and higher ratings.
  • Compliance: ASTM D1785 and NSF standards for potable water.
  • UV resistance: limited. May require protective coatings for prolonged outdoor exposure.
