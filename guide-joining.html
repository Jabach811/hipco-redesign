Basic joining techniques

Solvent cement, threading, flanges, socket fusion, butt fusion, and the rest. How each method works and where it belongs.

Download the PDF

There are several recommended methods of joining thermoplastic pipe and fittings, each with its own advantages and limitations.

Solvent cementing

The most widely used method in Schedule 40 PVC, Schedule 80 PVC, and CPVC piping systems, as described in ASTM D-2855. The outside diameter of the pipe and the inside diameter of the fitting are primed, coated with special cement, and joined together. Knowledge of the principles of solvent cementing is essential to an excellent job.

Solvent cement is not a glue. It is a process that uses solvents and resin to chemically fuse the pipe and fitting together at a molecular level. The single most significant cause of improperly made or failed solvent cement joints is lack of solvent penetration or inadequate primer application.

The dos and don'ts of CPVC solvent cement welding (Corzan)

Threading

Schedule 80 PVC, CPVC, PVDF, and PP can be threaded with special pipe dies for mating with Schedule 80 fittings that have threaded connections. Because this method makes the system easy to disassemble, repair, and test, it is often used on temporary or take-down piping, as well as systems joining dissimilar materials. Threaded pipe must be derated by 50% from solvent-cemented systems. Threaded joints are not recommended for PP pressure applications.

Flanges

Used to assemble PVDF and polypropylene pipe and fittings for high-temperature, corrosive-service applications, and wherever a system must come apart for maintenance. See each manufacturer's data for recommended bolt torque and gasket selection.

Socket fusion

Used to assemble PVDF and polypropylene pipe and fittings for high-temperature, corrosive-service applications. The pipe end and fitting socket are heated on a tool and pushed together, fusing into one piece. See each manufacturer's data for recommended joining parameters.

Butt fusion

Used to connect all sizes of polypropylene (Proline), PVDF (Purad), and other larger-diameter materials. The two pipe ends are heated flat and pressed together. Butt fusion is an easy, efficient fusion method, especially in larger diameters.

IR (infrared) fusion

Improving upon conventional butt fusion, IR welding uses a non-contact method. It uses the critical welding parameters of heat soak time, changeover time, and joining force as found with butt fusion. By avoiding direct contact with the heating element, IR fusion produces a cleaner weld with more repeatable and smaller bead sizes. The result is a superior weld for high-purity applications.

HPF fusion

An electric socket fusion system that joins Purad PVDF piping components, providing a smooth internal surface.

Smooth inner bore (SIB)

State-of-the-art technology for sanitary piping construction. The smooth interior surface of the weld eliminates all beads, crevices, and intrusions into the fluid system. Materials cannot become entrapped, and the possibility of bacterial growth and contamination is virtually eliminated. SIB reduces pressure loss due to friction and improves system hydraulics. Available in Kynar (PVDF) and polypropylene.

Electrofusion

Electrofusion fittings are manufactured with an integral resistance wire, molded in place. The wire is electrically heated by a microprocessor-controlled unit, fusing and bonding the pipe to the fitting.

Mechanical joint

Mechanical-joint polypropylene and PVDF drainage systems are used extensively for accessible, smaller-sized piping. The joint consists of a seal ring and nut. It is quick and easy to install and can be disconnected just as easily, which makes it well suited to under-sink and under-counter piping.

Sanitary mechanical joint

The Sani-Tech division of Saint-Gobain Performance Plastics offers a sanitary mechanical joint similar to the Ladish tri-clamp systems found in pharmaceutical, food, and beverage plants. Rigid tubing and fittings are formed with a sanitary flange and gasket and joined with a special mechanical clamp.
